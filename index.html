Technology in School

October 4, 2026 by Jordan Alexander

Technology in school involves using digital devices, software, and online platforms—such as laptops, tablets, interactive whiteboards, and AI tools—to support teaching and learning Benefits include:Better Engagement: Interactive games, quizzes (like Kahoot), and virtual field trips make lessons more active and fun. Real-World Skills: Students learn to use tools like Google Docs, Canva, and productivity software that modern jobs demand. Differentiated Learning: Software can adapt to different learning styles and speeds, helping students practice literacy and numeracy at their own pace.

Challenges include: Open tabs, notifications, and excessive screen time can pull focus away from schoolwork. Inequality and Access: Not every student has reliable internet or a personal device at home. Privacy and Safety: Collecting student data raises concerns over digital privacy, cyberbullying, and data ownership. Mixed Results: Some long-term studies and education guidelines suggest that high device density does not automatically improve academic achievement if strong teaching routines are missing.

Benefits of Aloe Vera

October 5, 2026 by Jordan Alexander

Aloe vera is a medicinal plant with antioxidant and antibacterial properties. Aloe vera benefits can include reducing dental plaque, accelerating wound healing, improving skin barrier function, and managing blood sugar. The cosmetic, pharmaceutical, and food industries use aloe vera extensively. Aloe vera is known for its thick, pointed, and fleshy green leaves. Each leaf contains a slimy tissue that stores water, making the leaves thick. This water-filled tissue is the “gel” that people associate with aloe vera products. Tooth decay and diseases of the gum are very common health problems. One of the best ways to prevent these conditions is to reduce the buildup of plaque, or bacterial biofilms, on the teeth. In a studyTrusted Source of 152 school children ages 8 to 14 years, researchers compared an aloe vera mouthwash with the standard mouthwash ingredient chlorhexidine. After 4 weeks of use, the aloe vera mouth rinse decreased plaque, gingivitis, and salivary Streptococcus mutans (a plaque-producing bacterium), making it comparable to that of chlorhexidine.
